---
title: 'The matrix $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$'
level: undergraduate
values:
  determinant: '$\det A = -3$'
  minor: '$M_{11} = 2$, $M_{12} = -2$, $M_{13} = -3$'
  cofactor: '$C_{11} = 2$, $C_{12} = 2$, $C_{13} = -3$'
why:
  minor: |-
    The minors for the first row of the matrix

    $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$

    are the determinants of the $2 \times 2$ matrices left after deleting row $1$ and one column:

    $$M_{11} = \det \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix} = 2, \quad M_{12} = \det \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 6 \\ 7 & 10 \end{bmatrix} = -2, \quad M_{13} = \det \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 5 \\ 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix} = -3.$$
  cofactor: |-
    The cofactors for the first row of the matrix

    $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$

    are the minors $2$, $-2$, and $-3$ with the signs $+$, $-$, and $+$, so $C_{12} = -M_{12} = 2$, while $C_{11} = M_{11}$ and $C_{13} = M_{13}$.
  determinant: |-
    The determinant of the matrix

    $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$

    is the expansion along the first row, using the cofactors:

    $$\det A = 1 \cdot C_{11} + 2 \cdot C_{12} + 3 \cdot C_{13} = 1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot (-3) = -3.$$
---
For this example, the last entry in $A$ was chosen to be $10$ instead of $9$ so that the determinant is not $0$.
The matrix with the entries $1$ through $9$ in order is singular, because its third row is twice its second row minus its first row, so its determinant is $0$.
