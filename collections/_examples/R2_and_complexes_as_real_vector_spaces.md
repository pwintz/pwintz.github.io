---
title: '$\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$, as real vector spaces'
level: undergraduate
is: [
  isomorphic,
  identified,
  equivalent
]
is_not: [equal]
why:
  identified: >-
    $\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are often identified through the linear bijection $(a, b) \mapsto a + b i$.
  equal: >-
    $\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are not equal, because their elements are different objects.
  equivalent: >-
    $\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are equivalent as real vector spaces, because they are isomorphic.
---
