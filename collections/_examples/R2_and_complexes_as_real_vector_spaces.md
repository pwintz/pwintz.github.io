title: '$\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$, as real vector spaces'
level: undergraduate
is: [isomorphic, identified, equivalent]
is_not: [equal]
why:
  identified: >-
    $\reals^2$ is often identified with $\mathbb{C}$ via $(a, b) \leftrightarrow a + b i$.
  equal: >-
    $\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are not equal, because their elements are different objects.
  equivalent: >-
    $\reals^2$ and $\mathbb{C}$ are isomorphic as real vector spaces, and so equivalent, because $(a, b) \mapsto a + b i$ is a linear bijection, and they are often identified through it.
