title: 'Cartesian coordinates $(x, y)$ on a plane, given by an origin $O$ and two perpendicular unit vectors $e_1$ and $e_2$'
level: high-school
is: [coordinate_system]
is_not: [vector_basis]
why:
  coordinate_system: >-
    Cartesian coordinates form a coordinate system on the plane, because each point $p$ gets the unique pair $(x, y)$ such that the arrow from $O$ to $p$ is $x e_1 + y e_2$.
  vector_basis: >-
    Cartesian coordinates are not a basis, because they are a rule for labeling points, not a set of vectors.
    They are built from the basis $(e_1, e_2)$ together with the origin $O$.
