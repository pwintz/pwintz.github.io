title: 'Choosing $2$ of the $3$ letters $A$, $B$, and $C$'
level: high-school
values:
  permutation: '$P(3, 2) = 6$: $AB$, $BA$, $AC$, $CA$, $BC$, $CB$'
  combination: '$\binom{3}{2} = 3$: $\setdef{A, B}$, $\setdef{A, C}$, $\setdef{B, C}$'
why:
  permutation: >-
    Choosing $2$ of the letters $A$, $B$, $C$ in order gives $P(3, 2) = 3 \cdot 2 = 6$ permutations, because there are $3$ choices for the first letter and then $2$ for the second.
  combination: >-
    Choosing $2$ of the letters $A$, $B$, $C$ without order gives $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ combinations, because each combination, such as $\setdef{A, B}$, comes from $2! = 2$ permutations, such as $AB$ and $BA$.
