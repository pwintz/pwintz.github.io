---
title: '$\pi$'
detail: "$\\pi = 3.14159\\ldots$ is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter"
level: elementary
is: [ratio, irrational_number, transcendental_number, real_number]
is_not: [rational_number, integer, algebraic_number]
why:
  ratio: >-
    $\pi$ is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.
  rational_number: >-
    $\pi$ is not a rational number, because it is not a ratio of two integers.
  real_number: >-
    $\pi$ is a real number, because it is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, a point on the number line.
  integer: >-
    $\pi$ is irrational, not an integer, and not algebraic, because it is transcendental.
  transcendental_number: >-
    $\pi$ is transcendental: Lindemann proved in 1882 that $\pi$ is not a root of any nonzero polynomial with integer coefficients.
---
