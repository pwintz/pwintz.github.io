---
title: 'The vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ in $\reals^2$'
level: high-school
is: [
  vector_basis,
  linearly_independent,
  spanning_set,
  orthogonal_vectors,
  pairwise_linearly_independent,
  orthonormal
]
why:
  vector_basis: >-
    The vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ form a basis of $\reals^2$, because every vector $(x, y)$ is $x (1, 0) + y (0, 1)$, in exactly one way.
    This basis is the _standard basis_.
  orthogonal_vectors: >-
    The vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ are orthogonal, because their dot product is $1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 1 = 0$.
  pairwise_linearly_independent: >-
    The vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ are pairwise linearly independent, because neither vector is a scalar multiple of the other.
  orthonormal: >-
    The vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ are orthonormal, because both vectors have length $1$ and their dot product is $0$.
---
