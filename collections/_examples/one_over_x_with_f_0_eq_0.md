published: false
title: '$f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$, at $x = 0$'
level: high-school
is: [infinite_discontinuity]
is_not: [
  removable_discontinuity,
  jump_discontinuity,
  oscillating_discontinuity
]
why:
  removable_discontinuity: >-
    $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$, has no removable discontinuity at $0$, because $\frac{1}{x}$ has no limit as $x \to 0$.
  jump_discontinuity: >-
    $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$, has no jump discontinuity at $0$, because the one-sided limits at $0$ do not exist as numbers.
  infinite_discontinuity: >-
    $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$, has an infinite discontinuity at $0$, because $\frac{1}{x} \to \infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$ and $\frac{1}{x} \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^{-}$.
  oscillating_discontinuity: >-
    $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ for $x \neq 0$, $f(0) = 0$, has no oscillating discontinuity at $0$, because $f$ is unbounded near $0$ and moves steadily toward $\pm\infty$ instead of swinging.
---
