title: A $100$ Ω resistor in series with a $0.5$ H inductor in a $60$ Hz circuit
level: undergraduate
values:
  resistance: '$R = 100$ Ω'
  reactance: '$X \approx 188.5$ Ω'
  impedance: '$Z \approx 100 + 188.5 i$ Ω'
why:
  resistance: >-
    The resistance of a $100$ Ω resistor in series with a $0.5$ H inductor is $100$ Ω, which is the resistance of the resistor, because the ideal inductor has no resistance.
  reactance: >-
    The reactance of a $100$ Ω resistor in series with a $0.5$ H inductor at $60$ Hz is the reactance of the inductor, $2 \pi \cdot 60 \cdot 0.5 \approx 188.5$ Ω, because the resistor has no reactance.
  impedance: >-
    The impedance of a $100$ Ω resistor in series with a $0.5$ H inductor at $60$ Hz is the sum of the two impedances, $Z \approx 100 + 188.5 i$ Ω.
    The magnitude $\abs{Z} \approx 213.4$ Ω is the ratio of the voltage amplitude to the current amplitude, and the angle $\arg Z \approx 62^{\circ}$ is how far the voltage leads the current.
---
The magnitude of the impedance is not the sum of the resistance and the reactance: $\abs{Z} = \sqrt{R^2 + X^2} \approx 213.4$ Ω, not $288.5$ Ω.
