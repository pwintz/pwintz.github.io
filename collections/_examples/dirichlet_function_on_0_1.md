title: 'The indicator function of $\mathbb{Q}$ on $[0, 1]$: $f(x) = 1$ for rational $x$ and $f(x) = 0$ for irrational $x$'
level: undergraduate
values:
  riemann_integral: 'None'
  lebesgue_integral: '$\int_{[0, 1]} f \mathrm{d}\mu = 1 \cdot \mu(\mathbb{Q} \cap [0, 1]) = 0$'
why:
  riemann_integral: >-
    The indicator function of $\mathbb{Q}$ on $[0, 1]$ is not Riemann integrable, because every subinterval contains rational and irrational points, so Riemann sums can be $0$ or $1$ however fine the partition.
  lebesgue_integral: >-
    The Lebesgue integral of the indicator function of $\mathbb{Q}$ is $0$, because the rational numbers in $[0, 1]$ have measure $0$.
