title: Sugar and heat each spreading $1$ cm through still water at room temperature
level: high-school
description: >-
  The diffusion coefficient of sugar in water is about $5 \times 10^{-10}$ m²/s, and the thermal diffusivity of water is about $1.4 \times 10^{-7}$ m²/s.
values:
  particle_diffusion: '$t \approx 2$ days'
  heat_diffusion: '$t \approx 12$ minutes'
why:
  particle_diffusion: |-
    Sugar takes about $2$ days to spread $1$ cm through still water by particle diffusion, because the time to spread a distance $L$ is about

    $$t \approx \frac{L^2}{D} = \frac{(0.01 \text{ m})^2}{5 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}} = 2 \times 10^{5} \text{ s}.$$
  heat_diffusion: |-
    Heat takes about $12$ minutes to spread $1$ cm through still water by heat diffusion, because the time to spread a distance $L$ is about

    $$t \approx \frac{L^2}{\alpha} = \frac{(0.01 \text{ m})^2}{1.4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}} \approx 700 \text{ s}.$$
---
Both processes follow the same diffusion equation, but heat diffuses through water about $300$ times faster than sugar does.
In practice, stirring or convection mixes both much faster than diffusion.
