title: 'The decimal approximations $1, 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, \dots$ of $\sqrt{2}$, as a sequence in $\mathbb{Q}$'
level: undergraduate
is: [cauchy_sequence]
is_not: [convergent]
why:
  cauchy_sequence: >-
    The decimal approximations of $\sqrt{2}$ form a Cauchy sequence, because the $m$th and $n$th approximations, for $m, n \geq N$, agree in their first $N$ decimal places and so differ by less than $10^{-N}$.
  convergent: >-
    The decimal approximations of $\sqrt{2}$ do not converge in $\mathbb{Q}$, because their only possible limit, $\sqrt{2}$, is not rational.
  complete_metric_space: >-
    The decimal approximations of $\sqrt{2}$ are Cauchy but do not converge in $\mathbb{Q}$, because $\mathbb{Q}$ is not complete.
    In $\reals$, the same sequence converges to $\sqrt{2}$.
---
