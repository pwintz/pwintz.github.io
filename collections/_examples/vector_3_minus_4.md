---
title: 'The vector $x = (3, -4) \in \reals^2$'
level: high-school
values:
  euclidean_norm: '$\norm{x}_2 = \sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2} = 5$'
  one_norm: '$\norm{x}_1 = \abs{3} + \abs{-4} = 7$'
  infinity_norm: '$\norm{x}_{\infty} = \max(\abs{3}, \abs{-4}) = 4$'
why:
  euclidean_norm: >-
    The Euclidean norm of $(3, -4)$ is the length of the arrow from the origin, $5$.
  one_norm: >-
    The 1-norm of $(3, -4)$ is $7$, the distance walking $3$ units sideways and then $4$ units down, and its infinity norm, $4$, is the larger of the two moves.
---
