title: 'Polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ on the plane without the origin, such that $r > 0$ and $0 \leq \theta < 2 \pi$'
level: high-school
is: [coordinate_system]
is_not: [vector_basis]
why:
  coordinate_system: >-
    Polar coordinates form a coordinate system on the plane without the origin, because each point other than the origin has exactly one distance $r > 0$ from the origin and one angle $\theta$ in $[0, 2 \pi)$.
  vector_basis: >-
    Polar coordinates are not a basis, because they are a rule for labeling points, not a set of vectors.
    They come from no basis, because their coordinates are not linear: doubling a point doubles $r$ but leaves $\theta$ unchanged.
