---
title: '$\exists y \, (y > x)$'
level: undergraduate
is: [formula_logic]
is_not: [sentence_logic]
why:
  formula_logic: >-
    $\exists y \, (y > x)$ is a formula, because $\exists y \, (y > x)$ applies the quantifier $\exists y$ to the formula $y > x$.
  sentence_logic: >-
    $\exists y \, (y > x)$ is not a sentence, although $\exists y \, (y > x)$ has a quantifier: the quantifier binds $y$, but $x$ is free, so the truth of $\exists y \, (y > x)$ depends on the value of $x$ (for example, if $x$ and $y$ range over the natural numbers, then the formula is true for every $x$, and if $x$ and $y$ range over $\setdef{1, 2, 3}$, then the formula is false for $x = 3$).
---
