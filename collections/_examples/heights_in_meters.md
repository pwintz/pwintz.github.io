---
title: 'The heights $1.6$ m, $1.7$ m, and $1.8$ m'
level: high-school
values:
  variance: '$\sigma^2 = 1/150 \approx 0.0067$ m$^2$'
  standard_deviation: '$\sigma = \sqrt{6}/30 \approx 0.082$ m'
why:
  variance: |-
    The variance of the heights $1.6$, $1.7$, and $1.8$ m is the average of the squared differences from the mean $1.7$ m:

    $$\sigma^2 = \frac{(-0.1)^2 + 0^2 + 0.1^2}{3} = \frac{0.02}{3} = \frac{1}{150} \approx 0.0067 \ \mathrm{m}^2.$$

    The variance is in square meters, because the differences are squared.
  standard_deviation: |-
    The standard deviation of the heights $1.6$, $1.7$, and $1.8$ m is the square root of the variance:

    $$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{150}} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{30} \approx 0.082 \ \mathrm{m}.$$

    The standard deviation is in meters, like the heights.
