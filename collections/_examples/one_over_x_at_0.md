title: '$f(x) = 1/x$, at $x = 0$'
level: high-school
values:
  limit_of_function: '$\lim_{x \to 0} f(x)$ does not exist'
  left_limit: '$\lim_{x \to 0^{-}} f(x)$ does not exist'
  right_limit: '$\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} f(x)$ does not exist'
why:
  left_limit: >-
    The left limit of $f(x) = 1/x$ at $0$ does not exist, because $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^{-}$.
  right_limit: >-
    The right limit of $f(x) = 1/x$ at $0$ does not exist, because $f(x) \to \infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$.
    The values grow without bound, so they do not approach a number.
    "$\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} f(x) = \infty$" is shorthand for this.
  limit_of_function: >-
    $\frac{1}{x}$ tends to $-\infty$ from the left of $0$ and to $\infty$ from the right, so neither one-sided limit exists as a real number.
    So the limit at $0$ does not exist.
