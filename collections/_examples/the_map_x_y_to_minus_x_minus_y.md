title: 'The map $f(x, y) = (-x, -y)$ on $\reals^2$'
level: high-school
is_not: [reflection_geometry]
why:
  reflection_geometry: >-
    The map $f(x, y) = (-x, -y)$ is not a reflection of the plane, even though $f$ flips the signs of the coordinates, because $f$ is the rotation by $180^{\circ}$ about the origin.
    The only point that $f$ sends to itself is the origin, but a reflection of the plane sends every point of a line to itself.
---
In $\reals^3$, the map $(x, y, z) \mapsto (-x, -y, -z)$ is also not a reflection, but it does reverse orientation: the map is a reflection composed with a rotation.

Some authors call $f$ a _point reflection_ (a reflection through the origin), but a point reflection is not a reflection across a line or a hyperplane.
