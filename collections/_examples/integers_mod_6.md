---
title: 'The integers modulo $6$, $\setdef{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5}$, with addition and multiplication modulo $6$'
level: undergraduate
is: [
  group,
  ring
]
is_not: [field]
why:
  group: >-
    The integers modulo $6$ form a group under addition modulo $6$, because $0$ is an identity and $6 - n$ is the inverse of $n$.
  ring: >-
    The integers modulo $6$, with addition and multiplication modulo $6$, form a ring, because the rules of integer arithmetic still hold after taking remainders.
  field: >-
    The integers modulo $6$ do not form a field, because $2$ has no multiplicative inverse: $2 k$ modulo $6$ is $0$, $2$, or $4$, and none of these is $1$.
    Also, $2 \cdot 3 = 0$ modulo $6$, although neither $2$ nor $3$ is $0$.
---
