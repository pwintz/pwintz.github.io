---
title: 'The integers $\mathbb{Z}$, with addition and multiplication'
level: high-school
is: [
  group,
  ring
]
is_not: [field]
why:
  group: >-
    The integers form a group under addition, because adding $0$ changes nothing, adding $-n$ undoes adding $n$, and addition is associative.
  ring: >-
    The integers, with addition and multiplication, form a ring, because they form a commutative group under addition, multiplication is associative and has the identity $1$, and multiplication distributes over addition.
  field: >-
    The integers, with addition and multiplication, do not form a field, because most integers do not have multiplicative inverses. For example, $2$ has no multiplicative inverse in $\mathbb{Z}$ because $1/2$ is not an integer.
---
