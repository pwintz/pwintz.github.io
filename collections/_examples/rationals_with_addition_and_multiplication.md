---
title: 'The rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$, with addition and multiplication'
level: high-school
is: [
  group,
  ring,
  field
]
why:
  group: >-
    The rational numbers form a group under addition, because $0$ is an identity and $-q$ is the inverse of $q$.
  ring: >-
    The rational numbers, with addition and multiplication, form a ring, because they satisfy the usual rules of arithmetic, including the distributive law.
  field: >-
    The rational numbers, with addition and multiplication, form a field, because multiplication is commutative and every nonzero fraction $a/b$ has the inverse $b/a$.
---
