title: 'The vectors $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ in $\reals^2$'
level: high-school
is: [
  pairwise_linearly_independent,
  linearly_independent,
  orthogonal_vectors
]
is_not: [orthonormal]
why:
  pairwise_linearly_independent: >-
    The vectors $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ are pairwise linearly independent, because neither vector is a scalar multiple of the other.
  linearly_independent: >-
    The vectors $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ are linearly independent, because $a (2, 0) + b (0, 2) = (2a, 2b)$ is the zero vector only if $a = b = 0$.
  orthogonal_vectors: >-
    The vectors $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ are orthogonal, because their dot product is $2 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 2 = 0$.
  orthonormal: >-
    The vectors $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$ are not orthonormal, because both vectors have length $2$; dividing each vector by $2$ gives the orthonormal vectors $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$.
