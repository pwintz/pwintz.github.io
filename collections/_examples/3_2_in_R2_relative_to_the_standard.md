title: '$(3, 2)$ is the coordinate vector of $(3, 2) \in \reals^2$, relative to the standard basis'
level: high-school
is: [vector, coordinate_vector]
why:
  coordinate_vector: >-
    $(3, 2)$ is a vector in $\reals^2$, and it is its own coordinate vector relative to the standard basis, because $(3, 2) = 3 (1, 0) + 2 (0, 1)$.
