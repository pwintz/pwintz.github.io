title: 'The $1$-form $x \, \mathrm{d}y - y \, \mathrm{d}x$ on $\reals^2$'
level: undergraduate
is: [differential_form]
is_not: [covector]
why:
  differential_form: >-
    The $1$-form $x \, \mathrm{d}y - y \, \mathrm{d}x$ on $\reals^2$ is a differential form, because it assigns to each point $(x, y)$ the linear function $(v_1, v_2) \mapsto x v_2 - y v_1$ of one vector, which depends smoothly on the point.
  covector: >-
    The $1$-form $x \, \mathrm{d}y - y \, \mathrm{d}x$ on $\reals^2$ is not a single covector, because it assigns a different covector to each point of the plane.
    Its value at one point, such as the covector $v \mapsto v_2$ at $(1, 0)$, is a covector.
