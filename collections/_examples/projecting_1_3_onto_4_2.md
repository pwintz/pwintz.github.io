title: 'Projecting $\vect{x} = (1, 3)$ onto $\vect{v} = (4, 2)$ in $\reals^2$'
level: high-school
values:
  orthogonal_projection: '$\operatorname{proj}_{\vect{v}} \vect{x} = (2, 1)$'
why:
  orthogonal_projection: |-
    The orthogonal projection of $\vect{x} = (1, 3)$ onto $\vect{v} = (4, 2)$ is $(2, 1)$, because $\ip{\vect{x}}{\vect{v}} = (1)(4) + (3)(2) = 10$ and $\norm{\vect{v}}^2 = 4^2 + 2^2 = 20$, so

    $$\operatorname{proj}_{\vect{v}} \vect{x} = \frac{10}{20} \, (4, 2) = (2, 1).$$

    The difference $\vect{x} - (2, 1) = (-1, 2)$ is orthogonal to $\vect{v}$, because $(-1)(4) + (2)(2) = 0$.
