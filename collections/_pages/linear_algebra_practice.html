These tools take you through linear algebra problems one step at a time, with hints and checks. Each tool is a ladder of levels, from the first idea up to the full problem, so you can start where you are and work up.

How every tool works

Guided Learning
The tool picks a new random problem at the level you choose. After a couple of problems with no hints, it suggests the next level.
Design your own problems
Choose the sizes, bases, or entries yourself, or load an example.
Support, from Full to None
With Full support, the page works every step so you can read a worked example. With less support, you do more of the steps. With None, you solve the whole problem on paper and enter only the answers.
Check, Hint, and Show
Check your answer to a step before moving on. A hint says what to do next, and a second click gives a more specific hint. The tool counts hints, so you can see whether a skill is solid.
Your progress
A log at the bottom of the page records the levels you have finished, so you can pick up where you left off.

The tools are built for practicing by hand: have paper in front of you, and use the page to check each step.

Gaussian Elimination Practice

Row reduce a matrix one row operation at a time, with a record of every step and a checklist that shows which conditions still fail.

Skills you will practice

  • Choosing among the three row operations: replacement, scaling, and swapping
  • Computing the rows that result from a row operation, with integers and fractions
  • Row reducing a system or matrix to row echelon form (REF)
  • Continuing to reduced row echelon form (RREF)
  • Recognizing when a matrix is finished
  • Working up from 2 × 3 systems to 4 × 5 systems and 4 × 4 matrices

Try Gaussian Elimination Practice

REF/RREF Study Tool

Check whether a matrix is in row echelon form or reduced row echelon form, see exactly which conditions fail, and quiz yourself on random matrices.

Skills you will practice

  • Stating the conditions for REF and for RREF: zero rows at the bottom, leading entries moving right, leading entries equal to 1, and zeros above leading entries
  • Finding leading entries and zero rows
  • Deciding whether a matrix is REF, REF but not RREF, or neither
  • Reading the rank, the nullity, and the number of solutions of a system from an REF matrix
  • Working with augmented matrices of increasing size

Try the REF/RREF Study Tool

Matrix of a Linear Map Practice

Find the matrix of a linear map relative to bases, then use it. Maps on ℝn and on polynomials, with standard and nonstandard bases.

Skills you will practice

  • Applying a linear map to each basis vector
  • Finding the coordinates of a vector relative to a basis
  • Assembling the matrix A of T from the coordinate vectors of T(vj)
  • Using A to find the coordinates of T(x) from the coordinates of x
  • Finding the rank and the nullity of A
  • Deciding whether T is onto and whether T is one-to-one
  • Maps given by a formula, a matrix, a description, or their action on a basis
  • Maps between different dimensions, onto a plane or a line, and on polynomials

Try Matrix of a Linear Map Practice

Determinants

Find determinants in every standard way, and connect them to area, volume, and invertibility.

Skills you will practice

  • Finding the determinant of a 2 × 2 matrix, and the area of the parallelogram spanned by its columns
  • Finding the determinant of a triangular matrix as the product of its diagonal entries
  • Choosing the best row or column for a cofactor expansion, and finding minors and cofactor signs
  • Finding 3 × 3 and 4 × 4 determinants by cofactor expansion
  • Finding a determinant by row reduction, tracking the effect of swaps
  • Using the properties of determinants: transposes, scalar multiples, products, powers, and inverses
  • Deciding whether a matrix is singular, and reading orientation from the sign

Try Determinants

Projections and Least Squares

Work each problem step by step while it is drawn in the plane or in space, and rotate the 3D picture to see the geometry.

Skills you will practice

  • Projecting a vector onto a line with ⟨x, v⟩/‖v‖²
  • Finding the component of a vector orthogonal to a line
  • Projecting onto a plane with an orthogonal basis, after checking orthogonality
  • Building an orthogonal basis with Gram–Schmidt
  • Solving the normal equation ATAx = ATb for a least squares solution
  • Finding the projection Ax* onto the column space, and the residual
  • Fitting a line of best fit to data

Try Projections and Least Squares

Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Find eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and eigenspaces while a picture shows which vectors the matrix stretches without turning.

Skills you will practice

  • Checking whether a vector is an eigenvector, by computing Av and comparing it with v
  • Checking whether a number is an eigenvalue, using det(A − λI)
  • Reading the eigenvalues of a triangular matrix
  • Finding the characteristic polynomial of a 2 × 2 matrix and its roots
  • Finding an eigenvector for each eigenvalue by solving (A − λI)x = 0
  • Handling a repeated eigenvalue and deciding whether the matrix is diagonalizable
  • Finding complex eigenvalues
  • Finding the eigenvalues and a basis of each eigenspace of a 3 × 3 matrix

Try Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Complex Numbers

Practice the arithmetic that complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors need, with each problem drawn in the complex plane.

Skills you will practice

  • Finding powers of i
  • Adding, subtracting, and multiplying in the form a + bi
  • Finding the conjugate and the modulus
  • Dividing by multiplying by the conjugate of the denominator
  • Writing a complex number in polar form reiθ, with the angle
  • Multiplying and dividing in polar form
  • Finding powers with De Moivre’s theorem and converting back to a + bi

Try Complex Numbers

Explore, without answering questions

These tools are for building intuition before or after practicing. Vector Span, Linear Independence, Column Space, and Null Space lets you drag vectors and coefficients in 2D and 3D. Discrete-Time Linear Systems and Eigenvectors shows why the eigenvectors of A decide where the states of xk+1 = Axk go.
