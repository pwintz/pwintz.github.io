Instructions

Row reduce the matrix one row operation at a time. Choose an operation below and click Apply (or press Enter). There are three kinds:

  • Replace: add a multiple of one row to another, like R2 → R2 − 3R1 (enter −3 as the multiplier).
  • Scale: multiply a row by a nonzero number, like R2 → (1/2)R2.
  • Swap: exchange two rows, like R1 ↔ R3.

Multipliers can be integers, decimals, or fractions such as -3, 0.5, or 1/2. Leading entries are circled, and the checklist shows which conditions still fail. Every step is listed above the form; use Undo to take one back, or Edit to change any step (the steps after it are redone).

Choose how much Support the page gives, to practice one skill at a time:

  • Full: choose the row operations (the page computes the new rows).
  • High: compute the new rows (the page gives each row operation).
  • Partial: choose the row operations and compute the new rows.
  • Minimal: do both, and also recognize when the matrix is in RREF.
  • None: do the whole reduction by hand, and enter only the final matrix.

With high, partial, and minimal support, you compute the new rows. With minimal support, the page doesn't tell you whether the matrix is in REF or RREF: say so yourself when you think it is (saying so too early counts as a hint). With no support, you row reduce the whole matrix on paper and enter only the matrix you end with; the page checks it, but gives no guidance along the way. If you're stuck, go back to minimal support with the same matrix. Rows you compute aren't checked automatically, so a mistake carries forward as it would on paper. To check your work, use Check (before applying a step) or Check all steps; a check that finds a mistake counts as a hint. Stuck? Hint suggests what to aim for next, and you can then ask for a more specific hint. Your work is saved in this page's address, so you can reload the page or share a link.

Guided Learning chooses the matrices for you, in levels from easy to hard: small systems to REF first, up to 4 × 5 systems to RREF. After two matrices at a level with no hints, the page suggests the next level, and after three with the same support, less support. To pick the size and goal yourself, or to type in your own matrix, choose Design your own problems. Your progress, at the bottom of the page, shows your mastery of each level and a log of recent matrices.

To practice telling whether a matrix is in REF or RREF, try the REF/RREF Study Tool.
