The projection of a vector $\vect{x}$ onto a subspace $W$ is the point of $W$ closest to $\vect{x}$. A least squares solution of $A\vect{x} = \vect{b}$ makes $A\vect{x}$ as close as possible to $\vect{b}$, so $A\vect{x}$ is the projection of $\vect{b}$ onto the column space of $A$. Fitting a line to data is a least squares problem with one equation for each data point. Work each problem below step by step while it is drawn in the plane or in space.

Instructions

The methods:

  • The projection of \(\vect{x}\) onto a nonzero vector \(\vect{v}\) is \(\operatorname{proj}_{\vect{v}} \vect{x} = \left( \ip{\vect{x}}{\vect{v}} / \norm{\vect{v}}^2 \right) \vect{v}\).
  • The projection onto a subspace \(W\) with an orthogonal basis \(\vect{u}_1, \ldots, \vect{u}_k\) is the sum of the projections onto the basis vectors. If the spanning vectors aren't orthogonal, Gram–Schmidt gives an orthogonal basis.
  • The least squares solutions of \(A\vect{x} = \vect{b}\) are the solutions of the normal equation \(A\trans A \vect{x} = A\trans \vect{b}\). Then \(A\vect{x}_*\) is the projection of \(\vect{b}\) onto \(\colspace{A}\).
  • A line \(y = c_0 + c_1 x\) of best fit is the least squares solution of the system with one equation \(c_0 + c_1 x_i = y_i\) for each data point.
