An eigenvector of a square matrix \(A\) is a nonzero vector \(\vect{x}\) that \(A\) stretches (or shrinks, or flips) without turning it off its line: \(A\vect{x} = \lambda \vect{x}\) for a number \(\lambda\), called an eigenvalue of \(A\). Below, practice finding eigenvalues and eigenvectors by hand, while the picture shows where \(A\) sends each vector.

Instructions

To find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of an \(n \times n\) matrix \(A\):

  1. Find the characteristic polynomial \(\det(A - \lambda I)\). For a \(2 \times 2\) matrix, it is \(\lambda^2 - (\operatorname{tr} A)\lambda + \det A\).
  2. The eigenvalues are its roots: the numbers \(\lambda\) with \(\det(A - \lambda I) = 0\).
  3. For each eigenvalue \(\lambda\), the eigenspace is the null space of \(A - \lambda I\): row reduce \(A - \lambda I\) and solve \((A - \lambda I)\vect{x} = \vect{0}\). Its nonzero vectors are the eigenvectors for \(\lambda\).
  4. \(A\) is diagonalizable when it has \(n\) linearly independent eigenvectors, that is, when the dimensions of its eigenspaces add up to \(n\).
