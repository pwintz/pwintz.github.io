A complex number has the form \(z = a + bi\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are {% if real_def %}real numbers{% else %}real numbers{% endif %} and \(i\) is the {% if imag_def %}imaginary unit{% else %}imaginary unit{% endif %} defined by \(i^2 = -1\). Each complex number is a point of the plane, with the real part \(a\) along the horizontal axis and the imaginary part \(b\) along the vertical axis. Instructions

The methods, for \(z = a + bi\) and \(w = c + di\):

  • Find a power of \(i\) one power at a time, starting from \(i^0 = 1\), \(i^1 = i\), and \(i^2 = -1\): each power is the one before it times \(i\). The powers repeat every four steps, so for a large power \(n\), only the remainder of \(n\) divided by 4 matters.
  • Add or subtract the real parts and the imaginary parts separately: \(z + w = (a + c) + (b + d)i\).
  • Multiply by expanding the product, then replacing \(i^2\) with \(-1\): \(zw = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i\).
  • The {% if conj_def %}conjugate{% else %}conjugate{% endif %} of \(z\) is \(\overline{z} = a - bi\), and the modulus of \(z\) is \(\abs{z} = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\), which satisfies \(z\overline{z} = \abs{z}^2\).
  • Divide by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator: \(z/w = z\overline{w} / \abs{w}^2\).
  • In polar form, \(z = re^{i\theta}\), where \(r = \abs{z}\) and \(\theta\) is the angle from the positive real axis to \(z\). Multiplying multiplies the moduli and adds the angles, so \(\left(re^{i\theta}\right)^n = r^n e^{in\theta}\) (De Moivre's theorem).

Support sets how much of each problem the page works for you. With full support, the page works every step, as a worked example.
