What these notation settings change

  • Only the math changes. Words in the text, such as "range" and "null space," stay as they are.
  • Only notation can be chosen, not conventions that change what a statement means, such as whether \(0\) is a natural number or whether \(\subset\) allows equality.
  • When the text compares notations (for example, "some books write \(\operatorname{im} T\)"), those notations are shown as written.

Use this notation in LaTeX

This site writes its math with macros that name what each symbol means, such as \range{T} for the range of \(T\), rather than with a fixed notation, so that your choices above can change how the macros are displayed. You can use the same approach in your own LaTeX documents: copy the definitions below, which match your current choices, into your preamble. Then, in your document, write, for example, \range{T}, \nullspace{A}, and A\trans. For a compound argument, use the Of version, which adds parentheses where they're needed: \nullspaceOf{A - \lambda I}.
