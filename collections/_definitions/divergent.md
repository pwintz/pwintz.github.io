title: Divergent sequence
term: divergent
applies_to: sequences and series
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      Think of a list of numbers that goes on forever.
      The list is _divergent_ if its numbers do not settle down toward any one number.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [sequence](/dictionary/definitions/sequence) $(a_n)$ of numbers.
      The sequence $(a_n)$ is _divergent_ if, for every number $L$, there is a distance $\varepsilon > 0$ such that infinitely many terms $a_n$ are at least $\varepsilon$ away from $L$.
      For example, the sequence might grow without bound or oscillate.
properties:
  - >-
    A divergent [sequence](/dictionary/definitions/sequence) can be [bounded](/dictionary/definitions/bounded), such as $1, -1, 1, -1, \dots$ Every unbounded sequence diverges.
---
"Diverges to infinity" is a special case of diverging.
