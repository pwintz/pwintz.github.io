title: Transmission Coefficient
term: transmission coefficient
applies_to: boundaries between media
synonyms: ['$T$']
levels:
  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider a [plane wave](/dictionary/definitions/plane-wave) that reaches a flat boundary between two media at normal incidence.
      The _transmission coefficient_ of the boundary is the ratio of the [amplitude](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) $A_t$ of the [transmitted](/dictionary/definitions/transmission-wave) [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) to the amplitude $A_i$ of the incident wave, at the boundary:

      $$T = \frac{A_t}{A_i}.$$
properties:
  - >-
    For the pressure of a sound [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave), the transmission coefficient is

    $$T = \frac{2 Z_2}{Z_2 + Z_1},$$

    where $Z_1$ and $Z_2$ are the [characteristic acoustic impedances](/dictionary/definitions/characteristic-acoustic-impedance) of the first medium and the second medium.
  - >-
    The transmission coefficient of the pressure can be greater than $1$: sound passing from air into water has $T \approx 2$.
    The pressure doubles, but almost none of the [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) is transmitted, because the same pressure carries much less energy in water than in air.
  - >-
    The fraction of the incident [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) that is transmitted is

    $$\frac{Z_1}{Z_2} T^2 = \frac{4 Z_1 Z_2}{(Z_1 + Z_2)^2},$$

    which is the same in both directions across the boundary.
