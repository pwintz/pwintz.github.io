---
title: Probability
term: probability
applies_to: events
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      The _probability_ of something happening is a number from $0$ to $1$ that says how likely it is: $0$ means it cannot happen, and $1$ means it is certain.
  high-school:
    context: measure-theoretic probability
    text: |-
      Consider a random experiment, and collect every possible [outcome](/dictionary/definitions/outcome) of the experiment into a set, called the _sample space_.
      Think of the probability as a way of measuring the size of sets of outcomes, like area measures the size of regions, such that the whole sample space has size $1$.
      An [event](/dictionary/definitions/event) is a set of outcomes, and the _probability_ of an event is the size of the event.
      For example, if you roll a fair die, then the sample space is $\setdef{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}$, each outcome has size $\tfrac{1}{6}$, and the probability of the event "the roll is even," which is $\setdef{2, 4, 6}$, is the size $\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
  undergraduate:
    context: measure-theoretic probability
    text: |-
      Consider a [probability space](/dictionary/definitions/probability-space) $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$.
      The _probability_ of an [event](/dictionary/definitions/event) $A \in \mathcal{F}$ is the number $P(A)$ that the [probability measure](/dictionary/definitions/probability-measure) $P$ assigns to it.
---
