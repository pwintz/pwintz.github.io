title: Coherent Waves
term: coherent (waves)
applies_to: waves
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation).
      The waves are _coherent_ if the [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) difference between the waves stays constant over time.
properties:
  - |-
    Coherent [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) produce a steady pattern of [interference](/dictionary/definitions/interference), with points of [constructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/constructive-interference) and [destructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/destructive-interference) that stay in place.
  - |-
    When two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) are not coherent, the [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) difference changes randomly, so the [interference](/dictionary/definitions/interference) averages out, and the intensity of the sum is the sum of the intensities.
  - |-
    Two independent sources, such as two light bulbs, are usually not coherent.
    A [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) and a copy of the wave that is split off from the same source, such as an echo of the wave, are coherent if the delay between the wave and the copy is shorter than the _coherence time_, which is the time over which the [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) of the source stays predictable.
  - |-
    Lasers produce light with long coherence times, which is why lasers are used to show [interference](/dictionary/definitions/interference) patterns.
