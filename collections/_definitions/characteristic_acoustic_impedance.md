title: Characteristic Acoustic Impedance
term: characteristic acoustic impedance
applies_to: media that carry sound
synonyms: [characteristic impedance, '$\rho c$']
levels:
  undergraduate:
    context: acoustics
    text: |-
      Consider a medium, such as air or water, with density $\rho$, in which sound travels at speed $c$.
      The _characteristic acoustic impedance_ of the medium is

      $$Z = \rho c.$$
properties:
  - >-
    In a [plane wave](/dictionary/definitions/plane-wave) of sound, the ratio of the sound pressure to the particle velocity is the characteristic acoustic impedance of the medium.
  - >-
    Characteristic acoustic impedance is measured in rayls, where one rayl is one pascal second per meter.
  - >-
    The fraction of a sound [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that is [reflected](/dictionary/definitions/reflection-wave) at a boundary between two media depends on the characteristic acoustic impedances of the media, through the [reflection coefficient](/dictionary/definitions/reflection-coefficient) $R = (Z_2 - Z_1) / (Z_2 + Z_1)$.
---
The characteristic acoustic impedance is about $413$ rayl for air at $20^{\circ}$C and about $1.5 \times 10^{6}$ rayl for seawater.

The word "acoustic [impedance](/dictionary/definitions/impedance)" has several related meanings:

- The _specific acoustic impedance_ at a point is the ratio of the sound pressure to the particle velocity at that point, which is the characteristic acoustic impedance only for a [plane wave](/dictionary/definitions/plane-wave).
- The _acoustic impedance_ of a duct or an opening is the ratio of the sound pressure to the _volume velocity_, which is the particle velocity times the area of the duct.
