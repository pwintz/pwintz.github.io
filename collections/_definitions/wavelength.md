title: Wavelength
term: wavelength
applies_to: waves
synonyms: ['$\lambda$']
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      The _wavelength_ of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) is the distance from one peak of the wave to the next peak.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) $u(x, t)$ that travels along a line and repeats in space.
      The _wavelength_ of the wave is the smallest number $\lambda > 0$ such that

      $$u(x + \lambda, t) = u(x, t) \quad \text{for all } x \text{ and } t.$$
properties:
  - >-
    The wavelength $\lambda$, the [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) $f$, and the [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed) $c$ of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) are related by

    $$c = f \lambda.$$
  - >-
    When a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) passes into a medium with a different [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed), the [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) of the wave stays the same, so the wavelength changes in proportion to the wave speed.
  - >-
    The _wavenumber_ $k = 2\pi / \lambda$ is the number of radians of [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) per unit distance.
  - >-
    [Waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) interact most strongly with objects and openings whose sizes are comparable to the wavelength, which is why sound, with wavelengths of centimeters to meters, bends around doorways, but light, with wavelengths below a micrometer, does not.
---
Wavelength is measured in units of length, such as meters.
