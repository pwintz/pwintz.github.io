title: Absorption of a Wave
term: absorption (of a wave)
applies_to: waves
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Absorption_ is the soaking up of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave), such as sound or light, by a material, which turns the wave into heat.
      For example, a thick curtain absorbs sound, so a room with curtains has fewer echoes.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) traveling through a material, or reaching the surface of a material.
      _Absorption_ is the conversion of the [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) of the wave by the material into other forms of energy, usually heat, which makes the wave weaker.
properties:
  - >-
    In a uniform material, absorption makes the [amplitude](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) of a [plane wave](/dictionary/definitions/plane-wave) decrease exponentially, as $e^{-\alpha x}$ with distance $x$, where $\alpha$ is the _absorption coefficient_.
  - >-
    The absorption of sound in seawater increases quickly with [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation), so low-frequency sounds, such as the calls of large whales, travel much farther in the ocean than high-frequency sounds.
  - >-
    A [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) also becomes weaker as the wave spreads out from its source and by [scattering](/dictionary/definitions/scattering), but neither of these converts the [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) of the wave into heat, so neither is absorption.
