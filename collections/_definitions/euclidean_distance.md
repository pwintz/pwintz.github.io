---
title: Euclidean distance
term: Euclidean distance
applies_to: points
synonyms: [straight-line distance, '$d_2(p, q)$']
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider two points $p = (p_1, \dots, p_n)$ and $q = (q_1, \dots, q_n)$ in $\reals^n$.
      The _Euclidean distance_ between $p$ and $q$ is the length of the straight segment from $p$ to $q$:

      $$d_2(p, q) = \sqrt{(p_1 - q_1)^2 + \dots + (p_n - q_n)^2}.$$

  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider two points $p = (p_1, \dots, p_n)$ and $q = (q_1, \dots, q_n)$ in $\reals^n$.
      The _Euclidean distance_ between $p$ and $q$ is 

      $$d_2(p, q) = \norm{p - q}_2$$

      where $\norm{\cdot}$ indicates the [Euclidean norm](/definitions/euclidean-norm).
