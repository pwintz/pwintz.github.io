published: false
title: Diffraction
term: diffraction
applies_to: waves
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Diffraction_ is the spreading of a wave as the wave passes through an opening or around the edge of an obstacle.
      For example, ocean [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that pass through a narrow gap in a sea wall spread out in a half circle on the other side of the wall.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that passes through an opening or past the edge of an obstacle.
      _Diffraction_ is the bending and spreading of the wave into the region behind the obstacle that the wave would not reach if the wave traveled only in straight lines.
properties:
  - |-
    Diffraction is strongest when the opening or the obstacle has about the same size as the [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength), and is weak when the opening is much larger than the wavelength.
  - |-
    Sound diffracts around doorways and corners much more than light, because the [wavelengths](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength) of sound are millions of times longer than the wavelengths of light.
  - |-
    (Huygens' principle.)
    Each point of the opening acts as a new source of [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave), and diffraction is the [interference](/dictionary/definitions/interference) of the waves from these sources.
  - |-
    A [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) of [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength) $\lambda$ that passes through a slit of width $a$ spreads out so that the first direction of complete [destructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/destructive-interference) is at the [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) $\theta$ from straight ahead such that

    $$\sin \theta = \frac{\lambda}{a}.$$
  - |-
    Diffraction limits how finely a telescope, a camera, or an array of microphones or hydrophones can separate the directions of two sources: the smallest [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) that an aperture of width $D$ can resolve is about $\lambda / D$.
