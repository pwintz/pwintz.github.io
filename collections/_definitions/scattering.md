title: Scattering
term: scattering
applies_to: waves
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Scattering_ is the bouncing of parts of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) in many directions by many small objects, such as light bouncing off of the water droplets in fog.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that travels through a medium containing many small obstacles or irregularities, such as light in fog or sound in water containing bubbles and fish.
      _Scattering_ is the redirection of parts of the wave in many directions by the obstacles.
properties:
  - >-
    Scattering by objects much smaller than the [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength), called _Rayleigh scattering_, grows with the fourth power of the [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation).
    Air molecules scatter blue light more than red light, which makes the sky blue.
  - >-
    _Diffuse reflection_ is scattering by a rough surface, such as paper or a painted wall, which sends the reflected light in many directions.
  - >-
    Scattering changes the directions in which the [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) of the [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) travels, but, unlike [absorption](/dictionary/definitions/absorption-wave), does not convert the energy into heat.
  - >-
    The sound scattered back toward a sonar from the sea surface, the seafloor, and the water is called _reverberation_.
