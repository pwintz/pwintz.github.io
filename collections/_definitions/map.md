- "Map" is more common than "function" when the function preserves some structure, as in "[linear map](/dictionary/definitions/linear-map)" or "[continuous](/dictionary/definitions/continuous) map."
  "Linear map" is more common than "linear function," partly because "linear function" also means $x \mapsto m x + b$ in school algebra.
- "Map" is the usual word in topology and geometry, where the inputs and outputs are points of spaces, as in "a continuous map from a sphere to the plane."
- "Function" is the usual word when the inputs and outputs are numbers, as in "the function $f(x) = x^2$."
- Some authors reserve "function" for maps whose outputs are numbers (real or complex), and use "map" for everything else.
  This is a convention of style, not a different definition.
