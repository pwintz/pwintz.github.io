title: Dispersion of a Wave
term: dispersion (of a wave)
applies_to: waves and media
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Dispersion_ is the separation of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) into its different frequencies, such as the colors of light, because the different frequencies travel at different speeds.
      For example, a prism separates white light into the colors of the rainbow by dispersion.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a medium, such as glass or deep water, through which [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) of many frequencies can travel.
      The medium has _dispersion_ if the [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed) in the medium depends on the [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) of the wave, so that a wave made of several frequencies, such as white light, separates into its frequencies as the wave travels.
  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider a uniform medium in which each sinusoidal [plane wave](/dictionary/definitions/plane-wave) $\cos(k x - \omega t)$ with wavenumber $k$ has the angular [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) $\omega(k)$ given by the _dispersion relation_ of the medium.
      The medium has _dispersion_ if the [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) speed

      $$c_p(k) = \frac{\omega(k)}{k}$$

      is a nonconstant function of $k$.
properties:
  - >-
    In a medium with dispersion, a group of [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with nearby frequencies travels at the _group speed_ $\diff{\omega} / \diff{k}$, which differs from the [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) speed $\omega / k$ of each wave in the group.
    The [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) of the group travels at the group speed.
  - >-
    Dispersion stretches out a short pulse as the pulse travels, because the frequencies that make up the pulse arrive at different times.
    Dispersion in optical fibers limits how closely pulses of light can be spaced, which limits the data rate of the fiber.
  - >-
    The [index of refraction](/dictionary/definitions/index-of-refraction) of glass depends on the [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) of the light, so [refraction](/dictionary/definitions/refraction) bends each color by a different [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angle), which is how a prism and the raindrops of a rainbow separate colors.
  - >-
    [Waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) on deep water satisfy the dispersion relation $\omega^2 = g k$, where $g$ is the acceleration of gravity, so long waves travel faster than short waves.
    The long swells from a distant storm reach a beach before the short waves from the same storm.
  - >-
    Sound in air and light in a vacuum have almost no dispersion: every [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) travels at nearly the same speed, so music sounds the same from near and far.

### Other meanings of "dispersion"

- In statistics, the _dispersion_ of a data set or a distribution is how spread out the data set or the distribution is, as measured by the [variance](/dictionary/definitions/variance), the [standard deviation](/dictionary/definitions/standard-deviation), or the range.
- In chemistry, a _dispersion_ is a mixture of fine particles of one substance spread through another substance, such as milk or smoke.
