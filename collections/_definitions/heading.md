---
title: Heading
term: heading
applies_to: vehicles
synonyms: [compass heading]
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      The _heading_ of a ship, airplane, or car is the compass direction that its nose points, such as north or east.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a vehicle on or above the surface of the earth.
      The _heading_ of the vehicle is the [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) from north to the direction that the vehicle's nose points, measured clockwise and written as a number $\psi$ such that

      $$0^\circ \le \psi < 360^\circ.$$

      For example, north is $0^\circ$, east is $90^\circ$, south is $180^\circ$, and west is $270^\circ$.
properties:
  - |-
    The heading is the direction that the vehicle points, while its [course](/dictionary/definitions/course) (or track) is the direction that the vehicle moves.
    A crosswind or a current makes the course differ from the heading.
  - |-
    The bearing of an object is the direction from the vehicle to that object, measured the same way as the heading.
  - |-
    A heading is measured clockwise from north, but the standard [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) in mathematics is measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis, which points east.
    A heading $\psi$ is the standard angle $90^\circ - \psi$.
  - |-
    Headings wrap around: $360^\circ$ is the same heading as $0^\circ$.
    So the turn from a heading of $350^\circ$ to a heading of $10^\circ$ is $20^\circ$ clockwise, not $340^\circ$ counterclockwise, which the [signed angle](/dictionary/definitions/signed-angle) between the two directions gives.
  - |-
    In aerospace and robotics, the heading is the yaw [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) in the [orientation](/dictionary/definitions/orientation-rigid-body) of the vehicle.
---
