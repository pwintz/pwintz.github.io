title: Electrical Resistance
term: resistance
applies_to: electric circuits
synonyms: ['$R$']
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a component of an electric circuit, such as a wire or a resistor, with a constant voltage $V$ across the component and a constant current $I$ through the component.
      The _resistance_ of the component is the ratio of the voltage to the current:

      $$R = \frac{V}{I}.$$

      Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω).
  undergraduate:
    context: linear circuits
    text: |-
      Consider a linear electric circuit with two terminals and with [impedance](/dictionary/definitions/impedance) $Z(\omega)$ at angular [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) $\omega$.
      The _resistance_ of the circuit at frequency $\omega$ is the real part of the impedance:

      $$R(\omega) = \operatorname{Re} Z(\omega).$$
properties:
  - >-
    (Ohm's law.)
    A component obeys Ohm's law if the resistance $R = V / I$ is the same for every voltage, so the current is proportional to the voltage.
  - >-
    A component with resistance $R$ and current $I$ converts electrical [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) into heat at the rate $P = I^2 R$.
  - >-
    The resistance of a wire is $R = \rho L / A$, where $L$ is the length of the wire, $A$ is the area of its cross section, and $\rho$ is the _resistivity_ of the material.
  - >-
    Resistances in series add, and for resistances in parallel, the reciprocals add.
