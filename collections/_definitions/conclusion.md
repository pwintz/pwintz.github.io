title: Conclusion
term: conclusion
applies_to: arguments
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      The _conclusion_ of an [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic) is the statement that the argument claims follows from its [premises](/dictionary/definitions/premise).
      For example, in the argument "All humans are mortal; Socrates is human; therefore, Socrates is mortal," the conclusion is "Socrates is mortal."
  undergraduate:
    context: logic
    text: |-
      Consider an [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic)

      $$p_1, \; p_2, \; \dots, \; p_n \;\therefore\; q.$$

      The [proposition](/dictionary/definitions/proposition) $q$ is the _conclusion_ of the argument.
properties:
  - |-
    The conclusion of a [valid](/dictionary/definitions/valid-argument) [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic) need not be true, because a valid argument can have a false [premise](/dictionary/definitions/premise).
    The conclusion of a [sound](/dictionary/definitions/sound-argument) argument is true.
---
In the statement of a [theorem](/dictionary/definitions/theorem), the conclusion is the part after "then" in "if $P$, then $Q$."
