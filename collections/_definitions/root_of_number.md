---
published: false
title: Root of a Number
term: root (of a number)
applies_to: numbers
synonyms: ['$n$th root', square root, cube root]
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      Consider a number $b$.
      A _square root_ of $b$ is a number $a$ such that

      $$a \times a = b.$$
    caveat: |-
      A square root is one kind of root.
      Other roots multiply the number by itself more times, such as a cube root, for which $a \times a \times a = b$.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a number $b$ and a positive [integer](/dictionary/definitions/integer) $n$.
      An *$n$th root* of $b$ is a number $a$ such that

      $$a^n = b.$$

      A $2$nd root is called a _square root_, and a $3$rd root is called a _cube root_.
properties:
  - |-
    The number $a$ is an $n$th root of $b$ if and only if $a$ is a root of the [polynomial](/dictionary/definitions/polynomial) $x^n - b$.
  - |-
    Every positive [real number](/dictionary/definitions/real-number) $b$ has exactly two real square roots, $\sqrt{b}$ and $-\sqrt{b}$.
  - |-
    Every nonzero complex number has exactly $n$ different complex $n$th roots.
---
The symbol $\sqrt{b}$ denotes only one of the square roots of $b$.
For a [real number](/dictionary/definitions/real-number) $b \geq 0$, the symbol $\sqrt{b}$ denotes the nonnegative square root of $b$, called the _principal square root_.
Similarly, $\sqrt[n]{b}$ denotes the principal $n$th root.
So "the square root of $9$" means $3$, while "a square root of $9$" can mean $3$ or $-3$.
