---
published: false
title: Index of Refraction
term: index of refraction
applies_to: transparent materials
synonyms: [refractive index, '$n$']
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a transparent material, such as water or glass.
      The _index of refraction_ of the material is the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum, $c_0$, to the speed of light in the material, $c$:

      $$n = \frac{c_0}{c}.$$
properties:
  - |-
    (Snell's law.)
    Light that crosses a flat boundary from a material with index $n_1$ into a material with index $n_2$ is [refracted](/dictionary/definitions/refraction) so that $n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2$, where the [angles](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) are measured from the [normal vector](/dictionary/definitions/normal-vector) of the boundary.
  - |-
    Every ordinary transparent material has $n > 1$, because light travels more slowly in a material than in a vacuum.
  - |-
    The index of refraction depends on the [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength), usually decreasing as the wavelength increases, which is called [dispersion](/dictionary/definitions/dispersion-wave).
---
The index of refraction is about $1.0003$ for air, $1.33$ for water, and $1.5$ for common glass.

For other [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave), such as sound, the index of refraction is defined relative to a reference speed, $n = c_{\text{ref}} / c$.
