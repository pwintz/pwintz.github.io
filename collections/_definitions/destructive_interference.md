title: Destructive Interference
term: destructive interference
applies_to: waves
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Destructive interference_ happens when two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) meet with the peaks of one wave lined up with the troughs of the other wave, so that the waves cancel each other.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) that pass through the same point at the same time.
      The waves have _destructive interference_ at the point if the waves are out of [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) by half a cycle at the point, so that the peaks of each wave arrive with the troughs of the other wave and the amplitude of the sum is the difference of the [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) of the two waves.
  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) and with complex [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) $A_1 = \abs{A_1} e^{i \phi_1}$ and $A_2 = \abs{A_2} e^{i \phi_2}$ at a point.
      The waves have _destructive interference_ at the point if, for some [integer](/dictionary/definitions/integer) $k$,

      $$\phi_1 - \phi_2 = (2 k + 1) \pi.$$
properties:
  - >-
    At a point of destructive interference, the amplitude of the sum is $\abs{\abs{A_1} - \abs{A_2}}$, which is the smallest possible amplitude, and two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with equal [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) cancel completely.
  - >-
    Two sources that emit [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) of [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength) $\lambda$ in [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) have destructive interference at the points where the distances to the two sources differ by an odd multiple of $\lambda / 2$.
  - >-
    The [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) that is missing at points of destructive interference is found at points of [constructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/constructive-interference), so no energy is destroyed.
  - >-
    Noise-canceling headphones use destructive interference: the headphones play a sound that is a flipped copy of the outside noise, which cancels the noise at the ear.
