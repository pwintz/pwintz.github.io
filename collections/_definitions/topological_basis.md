---
title: Basis for a Topology
term: basis (of a topology)
applies_to: collections of open sets
synonyms: [base, topological basis]
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [topology](/dictionary/definitions/topology) on a set and a collection of its [open sets](/dictionary/definitions/open-set).
      The collection is a _basis_ for the topology if every open set can be built by taking a [union](/dictionary/definitions/set-union) of sets from the collection.
  undergraduate:
    context: topological spaces
    text: |-
      Consider a set $X$ with a [topology](/dictionary/definitions/topology) $\mathcal{T}$, and a collection $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ of [open sets](/dictionary/definitions/open-set).
      The collection $\mathcal{B}$ is a _basis_ for $\mathcal{T}$ if, for every open set $U$ and every point $x \in U$, there is a set $B \in \mathcal{B}$ such that

      $$x \in B \subseteq U.$$
  graduate:
    context: sets without a given topology
    text: |-
      Consider a set $X$ and a collection $\mathcal{B}$ of [subsets](/dictionary/definitions/subset) of $X$.
      The collection $\mathcal{B}$ is a _basis_ on $X$ if

      1. every point of $X$ is in at least one set in $\mathcal{B}$, and
      2. for all $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal{B}$ and every point $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$, there is a set $B_3 \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$.

      The _topology generated by_ $\mathcal{B}$ is the collection of all [unions](/dictionary/definitions/set-union) of sets in $\mathcal{B}$.
properties:
  - |-
    A collection $\mathcal{B}$ of [open sets](/dictionary/definitions/open-set) is a basis for $\mathcal{T}$ if and only if every open set is a [union](/dictionary/definitions/set-union) of sets in $\mathcal{B}$.
  - |-
    A [topology](/dictionary/definitions/topology) has many bases.
    Every topology $\mathcal{T}$ is a basis for itself, and adding [open sets](/dictionary/definitions/open-set) to a basis produces another basis.
  - |-
    The [open balls](/dictionary/definitions/open-ball) of a [metric space](/dictionary/definitions/metric-space) form a basis for its [topology](/dictionary/definitions/topology).
  - |-
    A function $f : X \to Y$ between topological spaces is [continuous](/dictionary/definitions/continuous) if and only if the [preimage](/dictionary/definitions/preimage) under $f$ of every set in a basis for the [topology](/dictionary/definitions/topology) of $Y$ is open in $X$.
  - |-
    A topological space is called [second countable](/dictionary/definitions/second-countable) if its [topology](/dictionary/definitions/topology) has a [countable](/dictionary/definitions/countable) basis.
---
"Base" and "basis" are both used for this concept.

A _subbasis_ for $\mathcal{T}$ is a collection of [open sets](/dictionary/definitions/open-set) such that the finite [intersections](/dictionary/definitions/set-intersection) of its sets form a basis for $\mathcal{T}$.
A _neighborhood basis_ at a point $x$ is a collection of [neighborhoods](/dictionary/definitions/neighborhood) of $x$ such that every neighborhood of $x$ contains one of them.
