---
title: Amplitude
term: amplitude
applies_to: oscillations and waves
synonyms: ['$A$']
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      The _amplitude_ of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) is how far the wave rises above its resting level at its peaks.
      For example, a loud sound has a larger amplitude than a quiet sound.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a quantity $y$ that oscillates as a sinusoid about a resting value of zero:

      $$y(t) = A \cos(\omega t + \phi),$$

      where $A \geq 0$.
      The _amplitude_ of the oscillation is $A$, which is the largest distance of $y(t)$ from the resting value.
properties:
  # - >-
  #   The _peak-to-peak amplitude_, from the lowest value to the highest value, is $2 A$.
  # - >-
  #   The root-mean-square value of the sinusoid $A \cos(\omega t + \phi)$ is $A / \sqrt{2}$.
  - |-
    The [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) carried by a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave), and the intensity of the wave, are proportional to the square of the amplitude.
  - |-
    The amplitude and the [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) of a sinusoid are combined in the complex amplitude $A e^{i \phi}$, which is called a [phasor](/dictionary/definitions/phasor).
---
The amplitude of a sound is often given in decibels, $20 \log_{10}(A / A_0)$, relative to a reference amplitude $A_0$.
