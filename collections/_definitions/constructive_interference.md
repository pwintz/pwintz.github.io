title: Constructive Interference
term: constructive interference
applies_to: waves
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Constructive interference_ happens when two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) meet with the peaks of one wave lined up with the peaks of the other wave, so that the waves add up to a bigger wave.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) that pass through the same point at the same time.
      The waves have _constructive interference_ at the point if the waves are in [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) at the point, so that the peaks of the two waves arrive together and the amplitude of the sum is the sum of the [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) of the two waves.
  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) and with complex [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) $A_1 = \abs{A_1} e^{i \phi_1}$ and $A_2 = \abs{A_2} e^{i \phi_2}$ at a point.
      The waves have _constructive interference_ at the point if, for some [integer](/dictionary/definitions/integer) $k$,

      $$\phi_1 - \phi_2 = 2 \pi k.$$
properties:
  - >-
    At a point of constructive interference, the amplitude of the sum is $\abs{A_1} + \abs{A_2}$, which is the largest possible amplitude, and two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with equal [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude) add to a wave with twice the amplitude and four times the intensity.
  - >-
    Two sources that emit [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) of [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength) $\lambda$ in [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) have constructive interference at the points where the distances to the two sources differ by an [integer](/dictionary/definitions/integer) multiple of $\lambda$.
  - >-
    The word "constructive" is also used more loosely for every [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) difference that makes the sum larger than either wave alone, such as a phase difference less than $\pi / 2$ for [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with equal [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude).
