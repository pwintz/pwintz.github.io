---
title: Associative operation
term: associative
applies_to: binary operations
synonyms: [associativity]
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      A way of combining numbers is _associative_ if, when you combine three numbers in a row, it doesn't matter which pair you combine first, such as $(2 + 3) + 4 = 2 + (3 + 4)$.
  undergraduate:
    context: abstract algebra
    text: |-
      Consider a set $S$ with a [binary operation](/dictionary/definitions/binary-operation) $\ast$.
      The operation $\ast$ is _associative_ if

      $$(a \ast b) \ast c = a \ast (b \ast c) \quad \text{for all } a, b, c \in S.$$
properties:
  - >-
    Addition and multiplication of [real numbers](/dictionary/definitions/real-number) are associative, and so are composition of functions and multiplication of matrices.
  - >-
    Subtraction is not associative: $(5 - 3) - 1 = 1$, but $5 - (3 - 1) = 3$.
  - >-
    Associativity is different from [commutativity](/dictionary/definitions/commutative): matrix multiplication is associative, but $A B$ and $B A$ can differ.
---
