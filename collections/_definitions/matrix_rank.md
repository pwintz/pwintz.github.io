title: Rank of a matrix
term: rank (of a matrix)
applies_to: matrices
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [matrix](/dictionary/definitions/matrix) and its rows, some of which may be combinations of the others.
      The _rank_ of the matrix is the number of independent rows (rows that are not combinations of the others).
      For every matrix, this equals the number of independent columns.
  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider a [matrix](/dictionary/definitions/matrix) and its [column space](/dictionary/definitions/column-space), the span of its columns.
      The _rank_ of the matrix is the dimension of its column space: the maximum number of [linearly independent](/dictionary/definitions/linearly-independent) columns.
properties:
  - >-
    The rank also equals the maximum number of [linearly independent](/dictionary/definitions/linearly-independent) rows (row rank equals column rank).
  - >-
    For an $m \times n$ [matrix](/dictionary/definitions/matrix) $A$, the rank plus the dimension of the [null space](/dictionary/definitions/kernel) of $A$ is $n$ (the rank–nullity theorem).
