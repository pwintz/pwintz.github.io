title: Phase of an Oscillation
term: phase (of an oscillation)
applies_to: oscillations and waves
synonyms: ['$\phi$']
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a quantity that oscillates as a sinusoid,

      $$y(t) = A \cos(\omega t + \phi).$$

      The _phase_ of the oscillation at time $t$ is the angle $\omega t + \phi$, which says where in its cycle the oscillation is, such as at a peak, at a trough, or crossing zero.
      The constant $\phi$, which is the phase at $t = 0$, is called the _phase offset_.
properties:
  - >-
    Phase is measured in radians or in degrees, and phases that differ by a multiple of $2\pi$ radians ($360^{\circ}$) describe the same point in the cycle.
  - >-
    The _phase difference_ between two sinusoids with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) is the same at every time.
    Two such sinusoids are _in phase_ if the phase difference is a multiple of $2\pi$, and _out of phase_ if the phase difference is an odd multiple of $\pi$.
  - >-
    Delaying a sinusoid with angular [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) $\omega$ by a time $\tau$ subtracts $\omega \tau$ from the phase.
  - >-
    The phase offset $\phi$ of $A \cos(\omega t + \phi)$ is the argument of the [phasor](/dictionary/definitions/phasor) $A e^{i \phi}$.
---
### Other meanings of "phase"

- The _phase_ of a complex number $z$ is the argument $\arg z$, which is the angle of $z$ from the positive real axis.
- A _phase_ of matter is a state such as solid, liquid, or gas.
- The _phase space_ of a dynamical system is the space of its states, and the [phase margin](/dictionary/definitions/phase-margin) of a feedback loop measures its robustness.
