---
title: Frequency (of an Oscillation)
term: frequency (of an oscillation)
applies_to: oscillations and waves
synonyms: ['$f$', '$\nu$']
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      Think about a motion that happens repeatedly, like a swinging swing or a vibrating guitar string.
      The _frequency_ of the motion is the number of times that it repeats each second.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a quantity that is [periodic](/dictionary/definitions/periodic) in time with period $T$, such as the air pressure at one point in a sound [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave).
      The _frequency_ of the quantity is the number of repetitions per unit time:

      $$f = \frac{1}{T}.$$

      Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz), where one hertz is one repetition per second.
properties:
  - |-
    The _angular frequency_ $\omega = 2 \pi f$ is the number of radians of [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) per unit time, so that a sinusoid with frequency $f$ is $A \cos(\omega t + \phi)$.
  - |-
    The frequency of a sound is heard as its pitch: the higher the frequency, the higher the pitch.
  # TODO: Maybe link stationary to the distinction about constant vs. stationary vs. uniform, ect.
  - |-
    When a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) is reflected or refracted by a stationary boundary, the frequency of the wave does not change.
  - |-
    (The Doppler effect.)
    When the source of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) and the observer move toward each other, the observer receives the wave at a higher frequency than the source emits, and when the source and the observer move apart, the observer receives a lower frequency.
---
### Other meanings of "frequency"

In statistics, the _frequency_ of a value in a data set is the number of times that the value occurs, and the _relative frequency_ is that number divided by the size of the data set.
