---
title: Angle (Geometric Figure)
term: angle (geometric figure)
applies_to: rays and vectors
synonyms: ['$\angle ABC$']
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      An _angle_ is the shape made by two rays that start at the same point.
      The point is called the _vertex_ of the angle, and the two rays are called the _sides_ of the angle.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      An _angle_ is a geometric figure that consists of two rays with a common endpoint.
      The common endpoint is called the _vertex_ of the angle, and the rays are called the _sides_ of the angle.

properties:
  - |-
    Given points $A$, $B$, and $C$, we write the angle with vertex $B$ and sides passing through $A$ and $C$ as $\angle ABC$.

  - |-
    The [angular measure](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) of an angle is the amount of rotation needed to turn one side of the angle onto the other side.
    The measure is given in degrees or in radians, where $360$ degrees equals $2\pi$ radians.
  - |-
    An angle does not depend on the order of the two sides, so $\angle ABC$ and $\angle CBA$ are the same angle with the same angular measure.
    This is in contrast to the [signed angle](/dictionary/definitions/signed-angle) which says whether the turn is counterclockwise (positive) or clockwise (negative).
  - |-
    The angle between two nonzero vectors $\vect{x}$ and $\vect{y}$ is the angle formed by the rays that start at the origin and point in the directions of $\vect{x}$ and $\vect{y}$.

  - |-
    An angle is a [right angle](/dictionary/definitions/right-angle) if its measure is $90^\circ$, _acute_ if its measure is less than $90^\circ$, _obtuse_ if its measure is between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, and _straight_ if its measure is $180^\circ$.
---
## Notes

- Outside of geometry, "angle" usually means the [angular measure](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) of an angle, as in "the angle $\theta = \pi/4$" or "the angle between $\vect{x}$ and $\vect{y}$ is $90^\circ$."
