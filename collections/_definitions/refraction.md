title: Refraction
term: refraction
applies_to: waves
levels:
  elementary:
    text: |-
      _Refraction_ is the bending of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave), such as light, when the wave passes from one material into another, such as from air into water.
      For example, a straw in a glass of water looks bent at the surface of the water, because the light from the straw bends as the light leaves the water.
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that crosses the boundary between two materials in which the wave travels at different speeds, such as light passing from air into glass.
      _Refraction_ is the change in the direction of the wave as the wave crosses the boundary.
properties:
  - |-
    (Snell's law.)
    Consider a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that crosses a flat boundary from a medium with [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed) $c_1$ into a medium with wave speed $c_2$.
    The [angle](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure) of incidence $\theta_1$ and the angle of refraction $\theta_2$, both measured from the [normal vector](/dictionary/definitions/normal-vector) of the boundary, satisfy

    $$\frac{\sin \theta_1}{c_1} = \frac{\sin \theta_2}{c_2}.$$
  - |-
    A [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that enters a medium in which the wave travels more slowly bends toward the [normal vector](/dictionary/definitions/normal-vector) of the boundary, and a wave that enters a medium in which the wave travels faster bends away from the normal vector.
  - |-
    A [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that hits a boundary at a [right angle](/dictionary/definitions/right-angle), along the [normal vector](/dictionary/definitions/normal-vector), is not bent, even though the wave changes speed.
  - |-
    The [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) of a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) does not change when the wave is refracted, so the [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength) changes in proportion to the [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed).
  - |-
    When the [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed) changes gradually, rather than at a boundary, a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) bends gradually along a curved path.
    For example, the speed of sound in the ocean depends on the temperature and the pressure, so sound bends toward the depth at which sound is slowest.
  - |-
    When the [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed) depends on the [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation), which is called [dispersion](/dictionary/definitions/dispersion-wave), [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) of different frequencies bend by different [angles](/dictionary/definitions/angular-measure).
    A prism separates white light into colors by dispersion.
---
### Index of refraction

In optics, Snell's law is usually written in terms of the [index of refraction](/dictionary/definitions/index-of-refraction) $n = c_0 / c$ of each medium, where $c_0$ is the speed of light in a vacuum and $c$ is the speed of light in the medium:

$$n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2.$$
