---
title: Linearly Independent Vectors
term: linearly independent
applies_to: sets of vectors
levels:
  high-school:
    text: |-
      Consider a list of [vectors](/dictionary/definitions/vector) $\vect{v}_1, \dots, \vect{v}_k$.
      The vectors are _linearly independent_ if none of them can be built from the others by stretching and adding. 
      That is, none of the vectors are a [linear combination](/dictionary/definitions/linear-combination) of the other vectors.
  undergraduate:
    text: |-
      Consider vectors $\vect{v}_1, \dots, \vect{v}_k$ in a [vector space](/dictionary/definitions/vector-space).
      The vectors are _linearly independent_ if the only solution of

      $$c_1 \vect{v}_1 + \dots + c_k \vect{v}_k = \vect{0}$$

      is $c_1 = \dots = c_k = 0$.
properties:
  - >-
    A set that contains the [zero vector](/dictionary/definitions/zero-vector) is not linearly independent, because $1 \cdot \vect{0} = \vect{0}$.
---
