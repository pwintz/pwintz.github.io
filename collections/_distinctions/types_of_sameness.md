summary: >
  Equal objects are the same object. The other words describe objects that are different but "the same in some respect,"
  and each one depends on what respect is meant.
terms:
  - equal
  - congruent
  - equivalent
  - isomorphic
  - identified
examples:
  - example: one_half_and_two_quarters
    why: >-
      They are the same number. In school, these are called "equivalent fractions," because the fractions are written differently.
  - example: 3_and_10
    why: >-
      They are congruent modulo $7$: they have the same remainder after division by $7$.
  - example: two_3_4_5_triangles_in_different_places
    why: >-
      A rotation and a translation move one onto the other, but they are different sets of points.
  - example: reals_squared_and_complexes_as_real_vector_spaces
    why: >-
      $(a, b) \mapsto a + b i$ is a linear bijection.
  - example: p_rightarrow_q_and_neg_q_rightarrow_neg_p
    why: >-
      A statement and its contrapositive are logically equivalent but are different statements.

## Notes

- "Similar" is another word in this family. Similar triangles have the same shape but possibly different sizes, and similar matrices $A$ and $B = P^{-1} A P$ represent the same linear map in different bases.
- Equivalence, congruence, isomorphism, and similarity are all equivalence relations, so each one splits objects into classes of "same in that respect."
