  Reflection, refraction, and diffraction each change the direction in which a [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) travels, and the three words sound alike.
  [Reflection](/dictionary/definitions/reflection-wave) turns a wave back from a boundary into the material that the wave came from.
  [Refraction](/dictionary/definitions/refraction) bends a wave that passes across a boundary into a material in which the wave travels at a different speed.
  [Diffraction](/dictionary/definitions/diffraction) spreads a wave around the edges of an obstacle or an opening, even when the wave stays in one material.

## Notes

- A [wave](/dictionary/definitions/wave) that reaches a boundary is usually split: part of the wave is reflected, and the rest is [transmitted](/dictionary/definitions/transmission-wave), with [refraction](/dictionary/definitions/refraction), or [absorbed](/dictionary/definitions/absorption-wave).
- Refraction needs a change in the [wave speed](/dictionary/definitions/wave-speed), and [diffraction](/dictionary/definitions/diffraction) needs an obstacle or an opening comparable in size to the [wavelength](/dictionary/definitions/wavelength).
- Many small obstacles redirect a wave in many directions, which is called [scattering](/dictionary/definitions/scattering).
