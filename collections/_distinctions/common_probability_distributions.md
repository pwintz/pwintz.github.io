summary: >
  A few named [probability distributions](/definitions/probability-distribution) model most simple random experiments, and the same experiment can produce random variables with several of the distributions.
  A [uniform distribution](/definitions/uniform-distribution) makes all values equally likely.
  A [Bernoulli distribution](/definitions/bernoulli-distribution) describes one success or failure, a [binomial distribution](/definitions/binomial-distribution) counts the successes in a fixed number of trials, and a [geometric distribution](/definitions/geometric-distribution) counts the trials until the first success.
  A [Poisson distribution](/definitions/poisson-distribution) counts the events in a fixed time when events happen independently at a constant rate, and an [exponential distribution](/definitions/exponential-distribution) gives the waiting time until the first such event.
  A [normal distribution](/definitions/normal-distribution) approximates sums and averages of many independent random variables.

## Notes

- The Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, and Poisson distributions are [discrete](/definitions/discrete-distribution), the exponential and normal distributions are [continuous](/definitions/continuous-distribution), and a uniform distribution can be either.
- The geometric distribution is the discrete counterpart of the exponential distribution: both describe waiting times, and both are memoryless.
