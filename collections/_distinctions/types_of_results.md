summary: >
  In mathematical writing, proved statements are labeled by their role.
  A theorem, a proposition, a lemma, and a corollary are all proved, and they differ only in importance and role: a theorem is a main result, a proposition is a lesser one, a lemma helps prove something else, and a corollary follows quickly from another result.
  A claim is a step inside a proof.
  A conjecture has not been proved.
terms:
  - conjecture
  - theorem
  - proposition_result
  - lemma
  - corollary
  - claim
examples:
  - example: pythagorean_theorem
    why: >-
      The Pythagorean theorem is a theorem, because it is a proved main result of plane geometry.
      It is not a conjecture, because it has a proof.
  - example: goldbach_conjecture
    why: >-
      Goldbach's conjecture is a conjecture, because it is believed true and checked for very large numbers, but it has no proof.
      So it is not a theorem.
  - example: fermats_last_theorem
    why: >-
      Fermat's Last Theorem is a theorem, because Wiles proved it in 1994.
      Before then, it was a conjecture despite its name, since Fermat left no proof.
  - example: zorns_lemma
    why: >-
      Zorn's lemma is called a lemma, but it is a major result, equivalent to the axiom of choice.
      Its name shows that the labels describe a result's role where it first appeared, not its importance.
  - example: zero_derivative_means_constant
    why: >-
      "A function with zero derivative on an interval is constant" is a corollary of the mean value theorem, because it follows by applying that theorem to two points of the interval.
      It is not a conjecture, because it is proved.
  - example: claim_sequence_is_bounded
    why: >-
      "The sequence $(a_n)$ is bounded" is a claim, because it is stated and proved as a step inside a larger proof.
      It is not a theorem, because it is not cited outside that proof.

## Notes

- The ranking of theorem, proposition, and lemma varies between authors; some papers have no propositions at all.
- "Proposition" as a label for a result is unrelated to a [proposition in logic](/definitions/proposition), a statement that is true or false.
