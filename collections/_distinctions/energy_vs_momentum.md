summary: >
  Kinetic energy and momentum each grow with the mass and the speed of a moving object, and each is conserved in an isolated system, so the two quantities are easy to confuse.
  [Momentum](/dictionary/definitions/momentum) $p = m v$ is a vector, proportional to the velocity, and the total momentum is conserved in every collision.
  [Energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) is a scalar; the kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2} m \norm{v}^2$ grows with the square of the speed, and in an inelastic collision some kinetic energy becomes heat, though the total energy is conserved.

## Notes

- [Energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) is measured in joules ($\mathrm{kg} \cdot \mathrm{m}^2 / \mathrm{s}^2$), and [momentum](/dictionary/definitions/momentum) in kilogram meters per second ($\mathrm{kg} \cdot \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}$), so the two quantities have different [physical dimensions](/dictionary/definitions/physical-dimension).
- A force $F$ acting over a time changes the momentum by $\int F \, \mathrm{d}t$ (the impulse), and acting over a distance changes the kinetic energy by $\int F \cdot \mathrm{d}x$ (the work).
