summary: >
  When two [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) with the same [frequency](/dictionary/definitions/frequency-oscillation) overlap, the waves add, and the result depends on their [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) difference at each point.
  [Constructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/constructive-interference) happens where the waves are in phase, so the amplitude of the sum is the sum of the [amplitudes](/dictionary/definitions/amplitude).
  [Destructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/destructive-interference) happens where the waves are half a cycle out of phase, so the amplitude of the sum is the difference of the amplitudes.

      A $440$ Hz tuning fork and a $442$ Hz tuning fork alternate between constructive interference and destructive interference, because the difference in frequencies makes the phase difference grow steadily, by one full cycle every half second.

## Notes

- The words also describe [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) differences between the two extremes: [interference](/dictionary/definitions/interference) is partly constructive or partly destructive when the [waves](/dictionary/definitions/wave) are neither in phase nor half a cycle out of phase.
- [Constructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/constructive-interference) and [destructive interference](/dictionary/definitions/destructive-interference) move [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) from place to place, but do not create or destroy energy.
- Both kinds of interference need [coherent](/dictionary/definitions/coherent) waves to form a steady pattern.
