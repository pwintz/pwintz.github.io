title: Derive vs. differentiate
kind: siblings

summary: >
  "Derive" and "derivative" share a root, so the two verbs are easy to mix up in calculus.
  To differentiate a function is to compute its derivative.
  To derive a result is to obtain it by reasoning.
  Despite the similar words, "deriving" has nothing to do with derivatives.
terms:
  - derive
  - differentiate
examples:
  - example: obtaining_the_quadratic_formula_by_completing_the
    why: >-
      Obtaining the quadratic formula is deriving, because it establishes a result by reasoning.
      It is not differentiating, because no derivative is involved.
  - example: computing_the_derivative_of_x_5_with_the_power
    why: >-
      Computing the derivative of $x^5$ is differentiating, because it applies a known rule to compute a derivative.
  - example: showing_that_the_derivative_of_sin_x_is_cos_x
    why: >-
      Showing that $\sin^{\prime} = \cos$ is differentiating, because it computes a derivative.
      It is also deriving, because it establishes a new result about derivatives from the limit definition.

## Notes

- "Derive the derivative" is grammatical but awkward.
  Prefer "differentiate $f$" or "compute $f^{\prime}$."
- "Derivate" is not standard English for "differentiate," though it resembles the word in several other languages.
