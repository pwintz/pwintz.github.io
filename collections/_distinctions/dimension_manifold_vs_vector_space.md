areas: [linear-algebra, geometry]
summary: >
  The dimension of a vector space and the dimension of a [manifold](/dictionary/definitions/manifold) both count the directions you can move in a space.
  The [dimension of a vector space](/dictionary/definitions/dimension-vector-space) is the number of vectors in a basis, and it depends on the field of scalars.
  The [dimension of a manifold](/dictionary/definitions/dimension-manifold) is the number of real coordinates needed near each point, and it applies to curved spaces, such as a [circle](/dictionary/definitions/circle), that are not [vector spaces](/dictionary/definitions/vector-space).
  For $\reals^n$, the two dimensions are equal.
