title: Discrete vs. continuous vs. mixed vs. singular distributions
kind: siblings
areas: [statistics]
summary: >
  The [probability distribution](/definitions/probability-distribution) of a real [random variable](/definitions/random-variable) is classified by where the distribution puts its probability.
  A [discrete distribution](/definitions/discrete-distribution) puts all of its probability on countably many single values, and a [continuous distribution](/definitions/continuous-distribution) spreads all of its probability by a density, so that every single value has probability $0$.
  A [mixed distribution](/definitions/mixed-distribution) combines a discrete part and a continuous part.
  A [singular distribution](/definitions/singular-distribution) gives every single value probability $0$ but has no density, because it puts all of its probability on a set of length $0$.
