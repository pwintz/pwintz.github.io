summary: >
  Every real number is either rational or irrational. Separately, every number is either algebraic or transcendental.
  Irrational does not mean transcendental: $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational but algebraic.
relation: integers ⊂ rational numbers ⊂ algebraic numbers, and transcendental numbers ⊂ irrational numbers (for real numbers).

  - example: one_half
    why: >-

  - example: sqrt_2
    why: >-
      It is not a ratio of integers, but it is a root of $x^2 - 2$.

    why: >-

  - example: i_eq_sqrt_minus_1
    why: >-
      It is a root of $x^2 + 1$, but it is not real, so it is neither rational nor irrational.

## Notes

- "Irrational" only applies to real numbers. A complex number such as $1 + i$ is neither rational nor irrational.
- The algebraic numbers are countable, so almost every real number is transcendental, even though it is hard to prove that any particular number (such as $\pi$) is transcendental.
