title: Types of symmetry
kind: family
areas: [geometry, linear-algebra]
status: draft
summary: >
  "Symmetric" always means "unchanged by some swap or transformation," but what is swapped differs:
  points of a shape, $x$ and $-x$, rows and columns, or the two sides of a relation.
terms:
  - geometric_symmetry
  - even_function
  - symmetric_matrix
  - symmetric_relation
examples:
  - example: a_square
    why: >-
      It has $8$ symmetries, including rotations by multiples of $90°$ and $4$ reflections.
  - example: x_to_cos_x
    why: >-
      $\cos(-x) = \cos x$.
  - example: x_to_x_cubed
    why: >-
      It is odd, not even.
  - example: begin_bmatrix_1_2_2_3_end_bmatrix
    why: >-
      It equals its transpose.
  - example: is_a_sibling_of
    why: >-
      If $a$ is a sibling of $b$, then $b$ is a sibling of $a$.
  - example: leq_on_reals
    why: >-
      $1 \leq 2$ but not $2 \leq 1$. It is antisymmetric.
