title: Types of limits
kind: family
areas: [analysis]
status: draft
summary: >
  A sequence might not have a limit, but it always has a limit superior and a limit inferior (possibly infinite).
  A limit point belongs to a set, not a sequence.
terms:
  - limit_of_sequence
  - limsup
  - liminf
  - limit_point
examples_note: >-
  Each cell is the ___ of the sequence or set in that row.
examples:
  - example: sequence_one_over_n
    why: >-
      When the limit exists, it equals both the limit superior and the limit inferior.
  - example: sequence_alternating
    why: >-
      The terms keep returning to both $1$ and $-1$, so there is no limit, but there are a largest and a smallest subsequential limit.
  - example: set_of_reciprocals
    why: >-
      Points of $S$ get arbitrarily close to $0$, though $0 \notin S$. Each point of $S$ is isolated.
