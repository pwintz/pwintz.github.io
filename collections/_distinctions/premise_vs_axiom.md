summary: >
  Premises and axioms are both statements that are used without being proved, but they belong to different things.
  A [premise](/dictionary/definitions/premise) belongs to one [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic): it is one of the statements that the argument starts from.
  An [axiom](/dictionary/definitions/axiom) belongs to a whole theory: it is a statement that every proof in the theory may use without proving it.

## Notes

- Within a proof, the axioms of the theory are used as [premises](/dictionary/definitions/premise), so an axiom can serve as a premise of many [arguments](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic).
- A premise is not claimed to be true: a [valid](/dictionary/definitions/valid-argument) argument can have false premises.
  An axiom is also not claimed to be true about the world, but the axiom is accepted as true within its theory.
