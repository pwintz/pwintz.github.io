  Resistance, reactance, and impedance are each measured in ohms and each describe how a circuit component opposes an alternating current.
  [Resistance](/dictionary/definitions/resistance) is the part of the opposition that converts electrical [energy](/dictionary/definitions/energy) into heat.
  [Reactance](/dictionary/definitions/reactance) is the part that stores energy and returns it each cycle, which shifts the current out of [phase](/dictionary/definitions/phase-wave) with the voltage.
  [Impedance](/dictionary/definitions/impedance) combines the two as the complex number $Z = R + i X$.

## Notes

- For direct current ($\omega = 0$), the [impedance](/dictionary/definitions/impedance) of a circuit of resistors is its [resistance](/dictionary/definitions/resistance), which is why resistance is often described as the special case of impedance with no [reactance](/dictionary/definitions/reactance).
- The reciprocal of the impedance is the [admittance](/dictionary/definitions/admittance), whose real part and imaginary part are the _conductance_ and the _susceptance_.
