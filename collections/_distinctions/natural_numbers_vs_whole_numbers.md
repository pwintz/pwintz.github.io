  "[Whole numbers](/definitions/whole-numbers)" and "natural numbers" are used synonymously by some others to indicate all [nonnegative integers](/definitions/natural-numbers-from-zero), $0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$.
  "Whole numbers" is the usual name in primary schools, and "natural numbers" (or "nonnegative integers") is the usual name in mathematics.
  "Natural numbers" is, however, used by some authors to indicate the non-negative [integers](/definitions/integer), $1, 2, 3, \dots$ and "whole numbers" is used to indicate all integers, "$\dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots$.

## Notes

- The two names are synonyms only when the natural numbers start at $0$.
  Many authors start them at $1$; see [natural numbers: starting at 0 or at 1](/distinctions/natural-numbers).
- "[Whole numbers](/definitions/whole-numbers)" also has rival meanings: some authors use it for all [integers](/definitions/integer), or for the [positive integers](/definitions/natural-numbers-from-one).
  In technical writing, "[nonnegative integers](/definitions/natural-numbers-from-zero)" or $$\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$$ is unambiguous.
