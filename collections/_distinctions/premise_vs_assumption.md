summary: >
  Premises and assumptions are both statements that are taken as given, but they belong to different things.
  A [premise](/dictionary/definitions/premise) belongs to an [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic): it is one of the statements that the argument starts from.
  An [assumption](/dictionary/definitions/assumption) belongs to a result, such as a theorem: it is a condition that the result requires of the objects it is about, such as "$f$ is [continuous](/dictionary/definitions/continuous)."

## Notes

- In the proof of a theorem "if $P$, then $Q$," the assumption $P$ is used as a [premise](/dictionary/definitions/premise): the proof is a [deduction](/dictionary/definitions/deduction) of $Q$ from the premise $P$.
- In a proof, "assume" can also introduce a temporary assumption, such as the assumption in a proof by contradiction, which is dropped at the end of that part of the proof.
