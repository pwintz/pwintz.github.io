title: Argument vs. Deduction
kind: siblings

summary: >
  An argument and a deduction both lead from [premises](/dictionary/definitions/premise) to a [conclusion](/dictionary/definitions/conclusion), but a deduction also shows the steps.
  An [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic) is the premises together with the conclusion, which claims that the conclusion follows from the premises.
  A [deduction](/dictionary/definitions/deduction) is a sequence of steps, each justified by a [rule of inference](/dictionary/definitions/rule-of-inference), that shows that the conclusion follows from the premises.

## Notes

- Every [deduction](/dictionary/definitions/deduction) of $\psi$ from [premises](/dictionary/definitions/premise) $\Gamma$ gives an [argument](/dictionary/definitions/argument-logic) with the premises $\Gamma$ and the [conclusion](/dictionary/definitions/conclusion) $\psi$, and with sound rules of inference that argument is [valid](/dictionary/definitions/valid-argument).
- An argument can be valid even if nobody has written a deduction for the argument.
