summary: >
  The mean, median, and mode are different ways to summarize the middle of data, and they can be far apart.
  "Average" usually means the mean.
  The geometric mean averages growth factors and ratios, and the expected value is the mean of a probability distribution rather than of data.
terms:
  - arithmetic_mean
  - average
  - median
  - mode
  - weighted_average
  - geometric_mean
  - expected_value
examples_note: >-
  Each cell is the ___ of the data or distribution in that row.
examples:
  - example: data_with_outlier
    why: >-
      The mean of the data is $21.6$, because the outlier $100$ pulls it far above most of the data.
      The median and mode are $2$, unaffected by the outlier, and the geometric mean is about $4.13$.
  - example: fair_die
    why: >-
      The expected value and the median of a fair die roll are both $3.5$, which is not a possible outcome.
      Every outcome is equally likely, so every outcome is a mode.
  - example: credit_weighted_grades
    why: >-
      The credit-weighted grade point average is $3.5$, because the A counts three times as much as the C.
      The unweighted mean of the two grades is $3.0$.
  - example: investment_growth
    why: >-
      The geometric mean of the growth factors is $4$ per year, which is the right average, because growing by $4$ twice gives the same total, $16$.
      The arithmetic mean, $5$ per year, would overstate the total growth as $25$.
